Air Flow Simulator

About this simulator

Draw a floor plan, set the weather, and see how air moves through your space, so you can try window, door and fan arrangements before moving furniture. It combines two layers: standard engineering airflow-network equations (the ASHRAE / NIST CONTAM approach) decide how much air comes in and out of each window and door, and a 2D fluid simulation decides where it goes inside.

It is an approximation, not engineering CFD. Use it to compare arrangements and spot problem areas; don't rely on individual numbers.

Known limitations

  • Top-down slice at seated height (3.6 ft). Rising warm air, layering near the ceiling and other up-and-down motion aren't simulated. Ceiling fans aren't included yet.
  • Turbulence is calibrated, not resolved. Patterns and rough speeds are meaningful; local speeds can be off by 20–50%. In a flat slice, the air a fan pulls in behind it looks stronger than in a real room.
  • Outside air pressure uses wall-averaged coefficients. Nearby buildings, trees and fences aren't modelled beyond the terrain setting.
  • Walls don't heat or cool the air, and there's no sun, people or appliance heat yet.
  • Fan ratings vary a lot between products. The built-in airflows are typical values; results are only as good as the fan inputs.
  • Wind is steady. Real wind gusts and shifts direction.
  • Every window and door is treated as reaching seated height. Openings that don't are marked with an orange “!”, and their effect on people is overstated.
  • Room totals are corrected, local values are approximate. The room's average temperature and fresh-air age follow the airflow-network numbers; how they're spread across the room comes from the 2D simulation, with a mixing level taken from measurements in real homes.

The physics, sources and validation results are documented in SCIENCE.md and VALIDATION.md.
